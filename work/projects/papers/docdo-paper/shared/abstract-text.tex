Benchmark accuracy and surgical-planning requirements remain separated by a persistent gap in deep learning based organ segmentation from CT. This structured review assesses the benchmark evidence for 3D CT organ segmentation and the requirements for translating it into surgical planning workflows. PubMed, arXiv, and Google Scholar were searched programmatically (the Semantic Scholar lane was rate-limited during execution), complemented by a large-scale OpenAlex identification lane; searches covered publications from 2015 to the September 2026 execution date, with search reporting following PRISMA-S. Screening was AI-assisted with a human validation audit, and data extraction was a single-pass large-language-model procedure that was not independently adjudicated. Of 273{,}115 deduplicated records (8{,}846 from API searches; 264{,}269 added by the OpenAlex lane), 7{,}370 passed pre-filtering and title/abstract screening retained 1{,}035 unique studies; full texts were obtained for 333 (32.2\%), and 236 studies were included (224 method papers, 9 framework/software studies, 3 benchmark papers). CNN encoders predominate (205 of 224 method studies, 91.5\%), with U-Net-family designs in 71.4\%, true 3D processing in 82.1\%, and a framework stratum (MONAI, MONAI Label, nnInteractive, VISTA3D) plus emerging neuro-symbolic and knowledge-guided methods extending the taxonomy. On the BTCV benchmark, auto-configured CNNs reach a mean Dice of 85.2\% across three methods versus 80.5\% for two transformer methods---a descriptive difference that does not support a general family ranking; implementation quality matters more than architectural novelty. Vascular and hollow-organ segmentation, domain shift, and limited code release (18.3\%) remain the primary barriers to translation, and benchmark evidence alone does not establish clinical readiness. Limitations include an English-only search, a 32.2\% full-text retrieval rate skewed toward openly accessible records, a scorer-tier change between review waves, and the single-pass extraction. We recommend maturity-tiered evaluation and phased adoption, beginning with task-specific validation of openly available toolchains such as nnU-Net or TotalSegmentator.
