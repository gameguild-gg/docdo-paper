Benchmark accuracy and surgical-planning requirements remain separated by a persistent gap in deep learning based organ segmentation from CT. This structured review assesses the benchmark evidence for 3D CT organ segmentation and the requirements for translating it into surgical planning workflows. Three databases (PubMed via Entrez E-utilities, arXiv via the official arXiv API, and Semantic Scholar via the Graph API) were searched for studies published between 2015 and January 2026, with search reporting following PRISMA-S. Screening was AI-assisted with a human validation audit, and data extraction was a single-pass large-language-model procedure that was not independently adjudicated. Fifty-two studies presenting deep learning methods for 3D organ segmentation from CT with quantitative evaluation on public benchmarks were included; formal meta-analysis was precluded by protocol heterogeneity. CNN encoders predominate (50 of 52 studies used CNN architectures exclusively, 43 of 52 employed U-Net-family designs, and 34 of 52 addressed multi-organ segmentation). On the BTCV benchmark, auto-configured CNNs reach a mean Dice of 85.2\% across three methods versus 80.5\% for two transformer methods---a descriptive difference that does not support a general family ranking; implementation quality matters more than architectural novelty. Vascular and hollow-organ segmentation, domain shift, and limited code release remain the primary barriers to translation, and benchmark evidence alone does not establish clinical readiness. Limitations include an English-only search, full-text retrieval for 63 of 161 candidates (39.1\%), and the single-pass extraction. We recommend maturity-tiered evaluation and phased adoption, beginning with task-specific validation of openly available toolchains such as nnU-Net or TotalSegmentator.
