\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Three-dimensional segmentation of abdominal organs and their vasculature from
computed tomography (CT) is the foundation of modern image-guided surgical
planning. Despite a decade of deep-learning progress, a 2025 systematic review
of 52 representative studies that I co-authored shows that performance has
plateaued around \textbf{82\,\% Dice} for parenchymal organs (nnU-Net,
Swin UNETR) and remains markedly lower (\textbf{65--75\,\% Dice}) for vascular
structures, where topology, sub-voxel geometry, and bifurcations dominate
clinical utility. In parallel, a decade of deployment at
\textit{DocDo} (1\,000+ patients, 10+ years) shows that deterministic,
interaction-driven pipelines built on the Image Foresting Transform
(Falc\~ao~\textit{et al.}, 2004) and on Aylward's orthogonal vessel tracking
(2002) remain unmatched in \emph{controllability} and \emph{auditability} for
surgeons, but are limited by manual cost design and topological fragility.

This thesis proposes a \textbf{hybrid deterministic--learning framework} that
re-formulates classical seeded-IFT segmentation and orthogonal vessel tracking
as \emph{differentiable layers} whose cost functions, stopping criteria, and
geometric priors are learned end-to-end from data, while their algorithmic
backbone preserves the guarantees and editability surgeons depend on.
Topology- and geometry-aware losses (clDice, persistent homology)
are introduced as a supporting work-package targeting the
vascular gap. An open-source 3D editor coupled to the framework, deployed
through DocDo as a free SaaS, enables a prospective clinical validation with
surgeons at Hospital S\~ao Marcos (Teresina, Brazil) measuring
\emph{time-to-segmentation}, NASA-TLX cognitive load, and System Usability
Scale (SUS). Foundation-model adaptation (MedSAM, SAM-Med3D), implicit neural
representations and diffusion-based segmentation are explored as parallel,
lower-risk avenues.

The plan covers \textbf{48 months} (Oct.~2025 -- Sep.~2029) organized in five
work packages, targets publications in IEEE Access, Medical Image Analysis and
MICCAI/MIDL, and culminates in an anonymized public dataset, a peer-reviewed
clinical validation, and an open-source release.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} medical image segmentation; computed tomography; vascular
segmentation; CNNs; transformers; topology-aware learning; foundation
models; surgical planning; human--computer interaction; clinical validation.

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Clinical motivation}

Minimally invasive abdominal surgery, partial nephrectomy, hepatic
resection, pancreatic surgery, splenectomy, depends critically on the
surgeon's pre-operative understanding of three-dimensional anatomy and, in
particular, on the spatial relationship between the lesion and the surrounding
vasculature. Patient-specific 3D models reconstructed from CT have been
repeatedly shown to reduce intra-operative complications, shorten warm-ischemia
time and improve resident training; yet the production of such models remains
a manual, expert-intensive task that does not scale to the volume of cases a
public hospital handles every week.

\subsection{Industrial context: a decade at DocDo}

The candidate is the founder and lead engineer of \textit{DocDo}, a
Brazilian medical-imaging company that has, over the last decade, produced
patient-specific 3D models for more than one thousand surgical cases. The
production pipeline currently relies on two deterministic algorithms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Seeded Image Foresting Transform} (Seeded IFT) for organs,
        regions and -- in current routine practice -- vessels; and
  \item the \emph{Orthogonal Vessel Tracker} of Aylward and Bullitt,
        implemented but kept outside the routine pipeline because of
        stability problems at bifurcations and on elliptical cross-sections.
\end{enumerate}
The clinical feedback gathered
over a decade has shaped a clear set of requirements that no purely
deep-learning solution currently satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Controllability:} a surgeon or operator must be able to correct any
        output in seconds and the algorithm must respect that correction.
  \item \textbf{Auditability:} every voxel decision must be traceable to an
        explicit, inspectable cost.
  \item \textbf{Robustness to acquisition variability:} the same protocol must
        run on a low-dose abdominal CT from an entry-level scanner and on a
        high-resolution multi-phase study.
  \item \textbf{Sub-voxel geometric accuracy} where it matters most
        (tumour--vessel margins, ostia, bifurcations).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Academic context: a saturated state of the art}

A systematic agentic review of 52 representative studies (2015--Jan.~2026) led by the
candidate and currently under preparation for publishing shows that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Convolutional architectures dominate the literature (98.1\,\% of
        reviewed works); 3D U-Net variants appear in 82.7\,\% of studies.
  \item Performance on parenchymal abdominal organs has saturated around
        \textbf{Dice~$\approx$~82.3\,\%} (nnU-Net) and
        \textbf{82.4\,\%} (Swin UNETR), with overlapping confidence intervals.
  \item Vascular structures lag at \textbf{Dice~$\approx$~65--75\,\%}, with
        topology errors (missed branches, broken centerlines) that geometric
        metrics under-report.
  \item Generalist foundation models (TotalSegmentator, MedSAM, SAM-Med3D)
        offer remarkable zero-shot coverage but are not yet competitive on
        vessels nor on surgeon-grade boundary precision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Thesis statement}

The thesis argues that the next clinically meaningful step in 3D abdominal
segmentation requires a principled \emph{fusion} of the deterministic,
interaction-aware backbone that already works in production with the
representational power of modern deep learning, evaluated on both geometric
overlap and surgeon-rated clinical utility, rather than a larger network or
a new transformer block.

\section{Research Context and State of the Art}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Deterministic backbone}

\textbf{Image Foresting Transform.}
The IFT~\cite{falcao2004ift} provides a general graph-search framework that
unifies watershed, fuzzy-connectedness, and seeded region-growing under a
single optimum-path forest formulation.  Given an image graph $G=(V,E)$ with
voxel vertices $V$ and neighborhood edges $E$, the seeded variant assigns
every voxel $t$ the label of its closest seed $s \in S$ under a user-defined
path-cost function:
\[
  \ell(t) = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{s\in S}\; C^*(s,t), \quad
  C^*(s,t) = \min_{\pi: s\to t}\, \max_{e\in\pi}\, w(e),
\]
where: $\ell(t)$ is the organ-class label assigned to voxel $t$;
$S$ is the set of user-placed seed voxels (one per foreground class);
$C^*(s,t)$ is the optimal (minimum bottleneck) path cost from seed $s$ to
voxel $t$; $\pi\colon s\to t$ denotes a path, i.e.\ an ordered sequence of
adjacent voxels connecting $s$ to $t$; $e\in\pi$ ranges over every
edge (adjacency pair) on that path; $w(e)$ encodes the intensity contrast
between the two endpoints of edge $e$ (a hand-crafted or learned scalar
greater than zero at strong boundaries); and the innermost $\max_{e\in\pi}$
selects the \emph{highest-contrast} edge on a given path -- the
\emph{bottleneck} -- so the algorithm routes labels through the
lowest-contrast path between seed and target.  Three clinically
interpretable hyper-parameters govern the DocDo implementation: an
intensity threshold (suppresses leaks through low-contrast boundaries), a
path-cost budget (controls expansion radius), and a maximum-iteration ceiling
(ensures real-time latency).  This design gives an operator direct,
semantics-rich control -- a property that end-to-end networks do not provide
by construction.

After a decade of deployment across more than 1\,000 surgical planning cases,
three operational limits are well-documented:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hand-crafted costs.} Edge weights are designed per organ and
        per contrast phase; every new acquisition protocol requires
        re-tuning, making large-scale generalization impractical.
  \item \textbf{Partial-volume sensitivity.} At thin-walled structures
        (renal papillae, vessel walls, bile duct), the integer-valued
        boundary metric leaks through sub-voxel transitions.
  \item \textbf{Multi-class scaling.} Concurrent labelling of seven or more
        anatomical classes (liver, kidney, pancreas, spleen, and their
        vasculature) requires sequential or competitive seeding strategies
        that scale poorly in time and memory.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Orthogonal vessel tracking.}
Aylward and Bullitt~\cite{aylward2002vessel} model vascular centerlines as
\emph{intensity ridges} in the CT volume.  Starting from a user seed, the
tracker iteratively:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item computes the Hessian of the image in the plane orthogonal to the
        current propagation direction;
  \item estimates vessel radius and orientation from the dominant eigenvector;
  \item fits a Gaussian cross-section model; and
  \item advances the seed along the ridge.
\end{enumerate}
The
algorithm produces a geometrically explicit centerline with per-point radius
estimates and is surgeon-friendly because every failure mode is
inspectable in 3D.

Its known failure modes are precisely the structures that dominate
preoperative planning for partial nephrectomy and right hepatectomy:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bifurcations.} The single-ridge model must split;
        the Hessian eigenstructure becomes ambiguous near branch points,
        causing the tracker to miss one daughter vessel or to oscillate.
  \item \textbf{Non-circular cross-sections.} Portal vein and renal vein
        segments are often elliptical or tortuous; the Gaussian fit
        diverges, halting propagation prematurely.
\end{itemize}
In the DocDo environment the tracker is implemented but excluded from the
routine pipeline for this reason, falling back to seeded-IFT for vessel
segmentation at the cost of topological fidelity.

\subsection{Deep-learning backbone}

The systematic review of 52 studies (2015--January~2026) conducted for this
thesis reveals a field shaped by a small number of architectural archetypes.

\textbf{Convolutional encoder-decoder networks.}
Ronneberger \textit{et al.}~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} introduced the U-Net
encoder-decoder with skip connections for 2D biomedical segmentation in 2015;
\c{c}i\c{c}ek \textit{et al.}~\cite{cicek20163dunet} extended it to full 3D
volumetric convolutions, and Milletari \textit{et al.}~\cite{milletari2016vnet}
proposed V-Net with Dice-loss training. The review finds that convolutional
architectures account for \textbf{98.1\,\%} of included works, with 3D U-Net
variants present in \textbf{82.7\,\%} of studies.

\textbf{Self-configuring CNNs.}
nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} eliminates manual architecture search by
inferring patch size, batch size, network topology, and normalization strategy
directly from dataset fingerprints.  It achieves \textbf{Dice $\approx$
82.3\,\%} on the BTCV multi-organ benchmark with no task-specific tuning and
remains the most reliable single-model baseline across parenchymal organs.

\textbf{Transformer hybrids.}
UNETR~\cite{hatamizadeh2022unetr} replaces the convolutional encoder with a
Vision Transformer operating on non-overlapping volume patches.
Swin UNETR~\cite{hatamizadeh2022swinunetr} introduces shifted-window
self-attention to reduce the quadratic cost of global attention, reaching
\textbf{Dice $\approx$ 82.4\,\%} on BTCV -- statistically indistinguishable
from nnU-Net.  Overlapping confidence intervals between the two paradigms
suggest a methodological ceiling for parenchymal organ segmentation.

\textbf{Generalist foundation models.}
TotalSegmentator~\cite{wasserthal2023totalsegmentator} covers 117 anatomical
structures through large-scale nnU-Net training and offers off-the-shelf
predictions for gross anatomy.  MedSAM~\cite{ma2024medsam} and
SAM-Med3D~\cite{wang2024sammed3d} extend the Segment Anything paradigm to
the medical domain, enabling prompt-driven segmentation without
organ-specific retraining.  In the reviewed studies these models score
below 70\,\% Dice on renal vasculature and underperform task-specific
baselines at margins and bifurcations.

\textbf{Performance ceiling.}
Across all 52 reviewed studies, parenchymal-organ Dice has saturated near
82\,\% regardless of architecture family; the vascular gap (65--75\,\% Dice
for renal and portal vessels) persists across every paradigm. The limiting
factor appears to be the mismatch between overlap metrics and the clinical
requirement for topologically correct, surgeon-editable delineations rather
than representational capacity.

\subsection{Topology- and geometry-aware learning}

Standard overlap metrics (Dice, IoU) are blind to topological errors: a
method that bridges a tubular branch scores well on Dice while introducing
a clinically critical false disconnection.  Two families of loss functions
directly penalize such errors, and their synergy with the deterministic
backbone is a central hypothesis of this thesis.

\textbf{Skeleton-based losses.}
clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice} augments the standard Dice loss with a
soft-skeleton Dice term computed on the morphological skeleton of both
prediction and ground truth.  It penalizes disconnected centerlines without
increasing inference cost and reduces connectivity errors by 20--40\,\%
relative to standard Dice on tubular structures (coronary arteries, retinal
vessels).  The extension to 3D abdominal vessels is straightforward, yet
none of the 52 reviewed studies applies clDice to this anatomical target.

\textbf{Persistent homology losses.}
Hu \textit{et al.}~\cite{hu2019topology} compute a differentiable
approximation to the Wasserstein distance between the persistence diagrams of
prediction and ground truth, enforcing the correct Betti numbers:
$\beta_0$ (connected components) and $\beta_1$ (independent loops).
Applied to abdominal vasculature these constraints target missed
bifurcations and spurious bridges.  Only eight of the 52 reviewed studies
use topology-aware losses, and none targets abdominal vessels --- a gap that
WP3 of this thesis addresses.

\textbf{Branch-aware centerline losses.}
A branch-aware centerline term, penalizing missing daughter vessels at
bifurcation points, can be coupled to the differentiable vessel tracker of
WP2, which provides explicit centerline and radius estimates as a byproduct
of its forward pass.

\subsection{Differentiable classical pipelines}

Making classical optimization algorithms end-to-end differentiable is an
active research direction that provides the theoretical foundation for WP2.
Graph-cut-based methods have been embedded in neural networks via LP
relaxations and sub-modular rounding; geodesic shortest paths have been
differentiated through regularized softmin over path costs; level-set
evolution has been parameterized as a neural ordinary differential equation.
These approaches replace discrete combinatorial decisions with continuous,
temperature-controlled surrogates that admit gradient flow.

For the Seeded IFT, the minimum-cost path value
$C^*(s,t) = \min_{\pi: s\to t} \max_{e\in\pi} w(e)$, where $w(e)$ is a
learnable edge weight produced by the CNN feature backbone, can be
\emph{softened} via an entropy-regularized softmin:
\[
  \tilde{C}^\tau(s,t)
  = -\tau \log \sum_{\pi: s\to t}
    \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\textstyle\sum_{e\in\pi} w(e)\Bigr),
\]
where: $\tilde{C}^\tau(s,t)$ is the temperature-regularized (soft)
approximation to the hard bottleneck cost $C^*(s,t)$; $\tau > 0$ is the
\emph{temperature} parameter, a positive scalar that controls sharpness ---
as $\tau \to 0^+$ the $\log\!\sum\!\exp$ expression collapses to the hard
minimum, exactly recovering $C^*(s,t)$, whereas larger $\tau$ spreads
credit across all paths and produces a smooth, globally differentiable
landscape; the outer $\sum_{\pi: s\to t}$ enumerates all paths in the image
graph from seed $s$ to voxel $t$; $\sum_{e\in\pi} w(e)$ accumulates the edge
weights along one candidate path; and $\exp(-\cdot/\tau)$ converts each
path's cumulative cost into a non-negative weight so that low-cost paths
dominate.  Because $w(e)$ is a learnable output of the CNN feature backbone,
back-propagating through $\tilde{C}^\tau$ trains the network to produce edge
weights that minimize the topology-aware loss defined in WP3.  Analogously,
the Gaussian cross-section fit in the orthogonal vessel tracker can be made
differentiable with respect to the learned Hessian response by replacing the
argmax orientation estimate with a soft-attention readout over the
eigenvector field.

No prior work has applied end-to-end
differentiation to both the Seeded IFT and the orthogonal vessel tracker.
This thesis trains their edge-cost networks jointly with the topology-aware
losses of WP3 and evaluates the resulting segmentations on standardized
abdominal CT benchmarks.

\subsection{Identified gaps}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Vascular gap.} Methods saturated at 65--75\,\% Dice with
        unmeasured topology errors.
  \item \textbf{Interaction gap.} Deep models are not designed for
        \emph{controllable} correction; classical pipelines are
        controllable but not data-driven.
  \item \textbf{Validation gap.} The literature reports geometric metrics in
        isolation; clinically meaningful endpoints (time-to-segmentation,
        cognitive load, specialist confidence) are almost absent.
  \item \textbf{Real-world generalization gap.} Public benchmarks
        (KiTS, BTCV, AMOS, MSD) do not reflect the acquisition heterogeneity
        of a regional Brazilian hospital PACS.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Statement}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Research questions}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1] Can a \emph{differentiable} formulation of the Seeded IFT and of
       orthogonal vessel tracking be trained end-to-end so as to match or
       exceed nnU-Net and Swin UNETR on abdominal organs while retaining the
       controllability and auditability of the classical algorithms?
  \item[RQ2] Do topology- and geometry-aware losses (clDice, persistent
       homology, branch-aware centerline loss) close a measurable fraction of
       the 65--75\,\% Dice vascular gap and reduce Betti-number errors on
       abdominal vasculature?
  \item[RQ3] Can foundation models (MedSAM, SAM-Med3D) be prompt-adapted to
       act as zero-shot priors for the hybrid framework, accelerating
       annotation under an active-learning protocol that compensates for the
       limited paired DICOM/STL legacy data at DocDo?
  \item[RQ4] Does the proposed framework, exposed to surgeons through an
       open-source 3D editor, produce a statistically significant reduction
       in \emph{time-to-segmentation} and cognitive load (NASA-TLX) relative
       to the current DocDo deterministic pipeline, while maintaining or
       improving surgeon-rated segmentation quality?
\end{description}

\subsection{Hypotheses}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item[H1] End-to-end learning of IFT cost functions yields equal or better
       parenchymal Dice than nnU-Net while preserving interactive editability
       in $\le 2$\,s per correction on commodity hardware.
  \item[H2] A topology-aware variant of the hybrid framework improves
       vascular Dice by at least 5\,p.p. and reduces Betti-0/Betti-1 errors
       by at least 30\,\% on abdominal vasculature.
  \item[H3] Prompted foundation-model priors reduce the annotation effort
       (annotator-minutes per case) by at least 40\,\% in an active-learning
       loop on the prospective S\~ao Marcos cohort.
  \item[H4] Surgeons using the proposed editor are at least 30\,\% faster
       than with the current deterministic-only pipeline, with non-inferior
       surgeon-rated quality (5-point Likert, one-sided non-inferiority
       margin of $0.5$).
\end{description}

\subsection{Objectives and contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[O1] A \textbf{differentiable Seeded-IFT layer} and a
        \textbf{differentiable orthogonal vessel tracker} implemented in C++,
        compiled to WebAssembly with WebGPU compute kernels, with
        learned costs and stopping criteria.
  \item[O2] A \textbf{topology- and geometry-aware vascular segmentation
        method} built on top of O1, evaluated on KiTS, BTCV, AMOS and a
        prospective S\~ao Marcos cohort.
  \item[O3] A \textbf{foundation-model adaptation protocol}
        (MedSAM/SAM-Med3D prompting + active learning) integrated with O1.
  \item[O4] An \textbf{open-source web-based 3D segmentation/visualization
        editor} implemented in C++ compiled to WebAssembly with WebGPU
        compute kernels, packages published on npm and GitHub, delivered as
        a free SaaS through DocDo, instrumented for clinical evaluation.
  \item[O5] A \textbf{prospective clinical validation} at Hospital S\~ao
        Marcos with surgeon-rated endpoints (time-to-segmentation, SUS,
        NASA-TLX, quality Likert).
  \item[O6] An \textbf{anonymized public dataset} (S\~ao Marcos subset) and
        a \textbf{surgical-planning-grade benchmark suite} including clDice,
        Betti errors and clinically motivated distance metrics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and out-of-scope}

\textbf{In scope:} abdominal CT (single- and multi-phase); kidney, liver,
pancreas, spleen and their primary vasculature; adult patients; retrospective
DocDo cohort (STL-derived weak labels) and prospective S\~ao Marcos cohort.
\textbf{Out of scope:} MRI, ultrasound, paediatric cases, intra-operative
imaging, real-time AR overlays, vessel haemodynamics simulation.

\section{Methodology}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Overall approach}

The work treats deterministic priors as components of the learning system
rather than as baselines to defeat. The Seeded IFT and the orthogonal vessel
tracker are expressed as differentiable computation graphs whose internal
costs and stopping criteria are learnable parameters, optimized jointly with
a convolutional / transformer feature extractor under topology-aware losses.

\subsection{Data}

\textbf{Public datasets:} KiTS21/23 (kidney + tumour), BTCV (multi-organ),
AMOS22 (multi-organ multi-modal), MSD (pancreas, spleen, liver), and
TotalSegmentator (broad anatomy). These provide the controlled benchmark for
RQ1--RQ3.

\textbf{Retrospective DocDo cohort:} 1\,000+ patient-specific STL meshes
produced over ten years. Source DICOM volumes have been deleted for most
historical cases; therefore this cohort is treated as a \emph{shape prior /
weak-label} resource (STL re-voxelization, prior distribution over organ
shapes, weak supervision for the foundation-model adaptation in O3) rather
than as a paired image--label set.

\textbf{Prospective S\~ao Marcos cohort:} new CT studies acquired and
anonymized at the source PACS under a formal CEP/CONEP protocol, annotated by
DocDo specialists in the proposed editor under an active-learning regime.
Target: at least 150 cases over the project lifetime.

\subsection{Evaluation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Geometric:} Dice, Jaccard, HD95, average symmetric surface
        distance.
  \item \textbf{Topological / vascular:} clDice, Betti-0 / Betti-1 errors,
        branch detection rate, centerline length recall.
  \item \textbf{Clinical:} time-to-segmentation, NASA-TLX, SUS,
        5-point Likert quality, edit-count per case.
  \item \textbf{Reliability:} test--retest on duplicated cases; inter-rater
        agreement on the S\~ao Marcos cohort.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational infrastructure}

Training will use a small-scale cluster of four NVIDIA GB10 nodes available
through Champlain College, complemented by local workstations for development
and by the DocDo production cluster for SaaS deployment.

\section{Work Plan}
%==============================================================================

The work is organized in \textbf{five work packages} over \textbf{48 months}
(Oct.~2025 -- Sep.~2029).

\subsection{WP1 -- Foundations, data \& ethics (M1--M12)}

\textbf{Tasks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{T1.1} Finalize the systematic review and submit it for
        publication.
  \item \textbf{T1.2} Curate and harmonize the public datasets into a single
        multi-organ training/evaluation split.
  \item \textbf{T1.3} Re-voxelize the retrospective DocDo STL cohort into a
        shape-prior dataset.
  \item \textbf{T1.4} Prepare and submit the CEP/CONEP ethics protocol for the
        prospective S\~ao Marcos cohort.
  \item \textbf{T1.5} Specify the editor's data model and annotation schema.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Deliverables.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{D1.1} Systematic-review paper submitted.
  \item \textbf{D1.2} Harmonized public-dataset release script.
  \item \textbf{D1.3} STL shape-prior dataset (internal).
  \item \textbf{D1.4} Approved ethics protocol.
  \item \textbf{D1.5} Annotation schema v1.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item Ethics approval delay -- mitigated by early submission and by the
        existence of fully public datasets sufficient for RQ1--RQ2.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{WP2 -- Differentiable hybrid method (M6--M24)}

\textbf{Tasks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{T2.1} C++ implementation of a differentiable Seeded-IFT
        layer (multi-source shortest-path forest with learnable edge costs and
        soft relaxations), engineered for high-performance cross-platform
        deployment.
  \item \textbf{T2.2} Differentiable orthogonal vessel tracker (learnable ridge
        response, learnable cross-section regressor, differentiable step
        control).
  \item \textbf{T2.3} Coupling with a 3D CNN/transformer feature backbone.
  \item \textbf{T2.4} Benchmark against nnU-Net and Swin UNETR on multi-organ
        public data.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Deliverables.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{D2.1} Open-source C++ library published as packages on npm
        and GitHub.
  \item \textbf{D2.2} First methodological paper (target: \textit{Medical Image
        Analysis}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item Numerical instability of the differentiable shortest-path relaxation --
        mitigated by an entropy-regularized softmin and by gradient clipping;
        fallback to straight-through estimators.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{WP3 -- Topology-aware vascular segmentation (M18--M36)}

\textbf{Tasks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{T3.1} Implement clDice and persistent homology losses for 3D
        vessels.
  \item \textbf{T3.2} Branch-aware centerline loss exploiting the differentiable
        tracker of WP2.
  \item \textbf{T3.3} Evaluation on abdominal vasculature (KiTS renal
        vasculature; in-house cohorts).
  \item \textbf{T3.4} Ablations isolating the contribution of each loss term.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Deliverables.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{D3.1} Vascular-segmentation paper (target: \textit{MICCAI}).
  \item \textbf{D3.2} Updated open-source release with topology losses.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item Topology losses are non-convex and slow -- mitigated by hierarchical /
        patch-wise computation and by curriculum learning.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{WP4 -- Foundation-model adaptation \& active learning (M24--M36)}

\textbf{Tasks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{T4.1} Prompt-tune MedSAM and SAM-Med3D using clicks/scribbles
        exported from the editor.
  \item \textbf{T4.2} Use the foundation model as a zero-shot prior to bootstrap
        annotation on the S\~ao Marcos cohort.
  \item \textbf{T4.3} Active-learning loop driven by predictive entropy and
        topology-error proxies.
  \item \textbf{T4.4} Compare against direct foundation-model use
        (TotalSegmentator) and against WP2/WP3 alone.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Deliverables.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{D4.1} Active-learning protocol report.
  \item \textbf{D4.2} Workshop / short paper (target: \textit{MIDL} or
        \textit{MICCAI} workshop).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item Foundation models may underperform on abdominal vessels -- contingency:
        restrict O3 to parenchymal organs and report negative findings.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{WP5 -- Clinical validation, editor, dataset \& dissertation (M30--M48)}

\textbf{Tasks.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{T5.1} Release the open-source web-based 3D editor v1.0,
        implemented in C++ compiled to WebAssembly with WebGPU compute
        kernels, with the hybrid framework integrated; publish packages on
        npm and GitHub.
  \item \textbf{T5.2} Conduct the prospective surgeon study at S\~ao Marcos
        (time-to-segmentation, NASA-TLX, SUS, quality Likert), pre-registered.
  \item \textbf{T5.3} Anonymise and release the S\~ao Marcos public dataset.
  \item \textbf{T5.4} Write and defend the dissertation.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Deliverables.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{D5.1} Open-source editor release.
  \item \textbf{D5.2} Clinical validation paper (target: \textit{IEEE Access}
        or \textit{MedIA}).
  \item \textbf{D5.3} Dataset paper (target: \textit{Scientific Data}).
  \item \textbf{D5.4} Dissertation.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Risks.}

\section{Ethics and Responsible Research}
%==============================================================================

The research uses retrospective and prospective human CT studies and entails
no genetic data, no minors, and no other vulnerable populations. The
prospective component is conducted exclusively in Brazil (Hospital S\~ao
Marcos, Teresina, Piau\'\i) and follows the CEP/CONEP route; no cross-border
transfer of identifiable data is foreseen -- only fully anonymized volumes
and meshes leave the source PACS. The DocDo platform and all related
intellectual property are owned by the candidate, which removes any third-
party IP conflict; research outputs derived from DocDo data will be released
under permissive open-source / open-data licences whenever ethically and
legally possible. A detailed self-assessment following the UTAD ethics
template is provided as an annex to this plan upon request.
